# Glossary

**Status:** Approved initial framework baseline.

## Framework language

**AI-Native Operating Framework**<br>
An open business operating framework and method for defining, documenting,
applying, and improving the standards and procedures through which people and AI
perform work.

**Operating framework**<br>
A coherent set of business principles, standards, responsibilities, and methods
used to organize how work is performed.

**Method**<br>
The repeatable approach used to apply and maintain the operating framework in
real business processes.

**Standards maintenance method**<br>
The six activities used to create and maintain standards and SOPs: Understand,
Document, Validate, Approve, Use, and Improve.

**Shared operating memory**<br>
The controlled, durable body of sources, context, decisions, work state,
evidence, handoffs, and lessons that allows authorized people and AI to
continue, verify, and improve work over time.

**Durable memory item**<br>
An identifiable unit of operating memory preserved beyond the immediate task or
conversation with enough context, ownership, status, sources, controls, and
maintenance meaning for an authorized reader to judge and use it correctly.

**Authoritative source**<br>
A source designated by the responsible business, policy, record, or domain
authority to govern a fact, requirement, decision, or current state within a
defined scope.

**Synthesis**<br>
A summary, explanation, comparison, index, or analysis derived from sources.
Synthesis supports understanding but does not silently replace or gain the
authority of its sources.

**Business standard**<br>
An agreed expectation for how an aspect of business work is defined, performed,
controlled, or evaluated.

**Business lifecycle**<br>
The sequence or cycle through which an organization conducts a type of work.
The framework applies standards to existing business lifecycles rather than
replacing them with a universal sequence.

**Framework concern**<br>
One of the six subjects the framework requires business operations to address:
Intent, Responsibility, Work, Control, Assurance, and Learning.

**Intent**<br>
The purpose, scope, expected outcomes, and governing requirements of business
work.

**Responsibility**<br>
The ownership, roles, authority, and accountability assigned to business work.

**Work**<br>
The inputs, activities, decisions, dependencies, handoffs, and outputs through
which a business outcome is produced.

**Control**<br>
The policies, approvals, risks, exceptions, escalation paths, stop conditions,
and recovery expectations that bound business work.

**Assurance**<br>
The completion criteria, quality expectations, checks, evidence, and review used
to determine whether work was performed properly.

**Learning**<br>
The review, feedback, evidence, and change practices through which standards and
SOPs remain accurate and useful.

**Accountable owner**<br>
The person who remains answerable for a business outcome and for the operating
standard governing the work.

**Organizational interoperability**<br>
The ability of people, teams, and AI to understand and participate in work
through shared business language, operating standards, and maintained shared
operating memory. It is not a technical protocol.

**Standard operating procedure (SOP)**<br>
The documented procedure for performing a recurring business process or
activity consistently.

**SOP content standard**<br>
The minimum business information every SOP must communicate. It governs content
quality without requiring a fixed document layout.

**Framework example**<br>
An illustrative business SOP showing how the framework applies in a particular
domain. An example explains the method without becoming a framework requirement
or a test of the framework.

**Example annotation**<br>
The concise context accompanying a framework example: its scenario, framework
concern mapping, provenance label, and domain-specific boundaries.

**Domain-validated example**<br>
An example reviewed by someone with relevant domain knowledge for the level of
real-world or professional accuracy it claims.

**AI-native**<br>
Describes business operations in which people and AI may both perform work
under the same business standards and SOPs. It does not require AI participation
in every process or activity, and human accountability remains explicit.

## Excluded technical language

Harnesses, protocols, adapters, schemas, machine representations, technical
conformance, model-memory mechanisms, embeddings, and retrieval architectures
are not framework concepts. Git, file trees, databases, document platforms,
knowledge graphs, and search systems are implementation choices. They must not
shape the framework's business requirements.
