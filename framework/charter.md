# AI-Native Operating Framework Charter

**Status:** Approved initial framework baseline<br>
**Owner:** Brad Groux<br>
**Effective date:** 2026-07-30

## Preamble

Organizations have always depended on shared principles, responsibilities,
standards, and procedures to perform work reliably. AI changes who may
participate in that work, but it does not remove the need for sound business
operations.

The AI-Native Operating Framework exists to help organizations define and
maintain clear operating standards for work performed by people and AI. It is a
business framework and method. Technology may use it, but technology does not
define it.

## Mission

Provide an open, practical operating framework for defining, documenting,
applying, and improving the standards and procedures through which people and AI
perform business work.

## Vision

Organizations operate coherently because their purpose, responsibilities, work,
controls, assurance, and learning practices are explicit and shared—regardless
of which people or technologies participate.

## Purpose

The framework establishes:

- the business concerns every process must make clear;
- the minimum content a trustworthy SOP must communicate;
- a standard for preserving and sharing operating knowledge across time and
  handoffs;
- a practical method for maintaining standards and SOPs;
- and examples showing how the same framework applies across different
  business domains.

It is intended to strengthen existing business lifecycles, not replace them.

## Scope

The framework applies to business work that may be performed by:

- people;
- AI;
- or people and AI together.

It applies across recurring operations, knowledge work, physical work,
regulated work, customer-facing work, cross-functional coordination, and
temporary or exceptional programs.

The framework concerns how work is understood, owned, performed, controlled,
verified, and improved.

## Founding Commitments

### Business operations come first

Business purpose, accountability, requirements, and outcomes determine how work
should operate. Technology choices do not.

### People and AI use the same standards

The framework does not create separate operating systems for human work and AI
work. People and AI use the same approved business standards and SOPs.

### Human accountability remains explicit

AI participation does not erase accountable human ownership of business
outcomes. Every process identifies who owns the work, who has decision
authority, and where escalation belongs.

### Shared understanding enables interoperability

Interoperability means that people, teams, and AI can understand and participate
in work through shared business language and operating standards. It is not a
technical protocol.

### Clear documentation serves every reader

The framework uses one canonical body of clear, complete, consistent, and
well-structured business documentation. People and machines use the same
material.

### Existing lifecycles are respected

The framework identifies what business operations must make explicit. It does
not impose one universal sequence on unlike processes or require organizations
to discard sound existing methods.

### Meaning matters more than templates

Standards and SOPs are judged by whether required business meaning is present
and clear, not by whether headings match a prescribed layout.

### Controls and evidence are part of the work

Authority, policy, risk, approval, exception handling, verification, and
evidence are part of operating well—not administrative material added after the
work is done.

### Operating knowledge must survive the moment

Material sources, context, decisions, work state, evidence, handoffs, and
lessons are captured and maintained under accountable controls so authorized
people and AI can continue and verify work. Individual recollection,
conversation history, and temporary model context are not sufficient
organizational memory.

### Standards must learn

Standards and SOPs are reviewed and improved using outcomes, evidence,
exceptions, incidents, practitioner knowledge, and changed requirements.

### The framework remains open and vendor-neutral

The framework is licensed under the [MIT License](../LICENSE.md), allowing
people and organizations to learn from, use, critique, adapt, and contribute to
it without adopting a particular vendor, model, platform, consultancy, or
product.

## Relationship to Education and Commercial Work

The framework is developed as part of
[Digital Meld](https://digitalmeld.io)'s research arm.
[AI Dev Days](https://github.com/bradgroux/ai-dev-days) is a related research
and education initiative within the same research arm. AI Dev Days may teach
the framework. Digital Meld and other organizations may apply it commercially.
Examples, educators, consultants, and service providers may contribute useful
experience.

None of them owns the meaning of the framework by virtue of teaching, applying,
or commercializing it. Changes to the framework follow its documented
stewardship and amendment process.

## Non-Goals

The framework does not:

- specify software, schemas, APIs, protocols, or machine-specific formats;
- define models, tools, harnesses, platforms, or integrations;
- create adapters or technical implementation profiles;
- prescribe a memory technology, repository layout, file format, retrieval
  method, or separate machine-memory system;
- prescribe one universal business lifecycle;
- require one SOP template;
- define a sales, marketing, consulting, or training methodology;
- require AI in every process or activity;
- replace professional, legal, regulatory, safety, or clinical judgment;
- certify conformance or claim to be an industry standard;
- or guarantee that a documented process is lawful, safe, effective, or
  appropriate for a particular organization.

## Stewardship

Brad Groux is the creator and founding steward of the framework. Founding
stewardship exists to maintain clarity, openness, and continuity while the
framework develops.

Stewardship must:

- preserve the charter's commitments;
- document material decisions and changes;
- distinguish framework requirements from examples and domain choices;
- disclose relevant commercial relationships;
- invite practical critique and contribution;
- and expand participation as the contributor community develops.

## Community Promise

The framework will:

- remain publicly inspectable;
- preserve attribution and contribution history;
- label uncertainty and review status honestly;
- distinguish illustrative examples from professional guidance;
- avoid hiding essential framework knowledge behind commercial services;
- and evolve through documented reasoning rather than silent reinterpretation.

## Amendment

A charter amendment requires:

1. a written proposal explaining the change and its consequences;
2. review against the framework's mission and founding commitments;
3. a documented decision by the responsible steward or future governing body;
4. publication of material dissent or unresolved concerns;
5. and a clear effective date and change history.

No example, implementation, educational program, marketing message, or
commercial practice may amend the charter implicitly.
