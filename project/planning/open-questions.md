## Resolved questions

### OQ-009 — Approve transition to framework specification?

**Decision:** Approved. Specification work may produce the charter, operating
framework, SOP standard, standards maintenance method, ten annotated examples,
and example-contribution SOP.

Approval does not authorize a technology platform, machine-specific
representation, marketing program, public launch, or publication.

Discovery established:

1. The framework is a business operating framework and method, not a technology
   specification.
2. People and AI may perform work under the same standards and SOPs while human
   accountability remains explicit.
3. The framework applies standards to existing business lifecycles rather than
   prescribing a universal lifecycle.
4. Six business concerns organize the framework: Intent, Responsibility, Work,
   Control, Assurance, and Learning.
5. SOPs have eight content requirements without a mandatory document layout.
6. Standards and SOPs are maintained through Understand, Document, Validate,
   Approve, Use, and Improve.
7. Ten complete, annotated examples illustrate the framework across domains.
8. A contribution SOP governs additional examples.

### OQ-008 — What must the example-contribution SOP require?

**Decision:** The contribution SOP governs example scope, provenance,
publication safety, SOP completeness, annotations, internal review, domain
review status, attribution, maintenance, and maintainer acceptance.

### OQ-007 — How complete should each example be?

**Decision:** Each example includes a scenario overview, a complete illustrative
SOP, a six-concern annotation, a provenance label, and a note separating
domain-specific choices from universal framework requirements.

The additional context improves understanding for people and machines without
creating a separate machine-oriented design.

### OQ-006 — Which examples should the framework include?

**Decision:** Include ten illustrative examples:

1. Accounts-payable invoice processing.
2. Software-change delivery.
3. Construction field-incident response.
4. Employee onboarding and offboarding.
5. M&A Day 1 transition.
6. Customer complaint and service recovery.
7. Regulatory-change implementation.
8. Supply-chain disruption response.
9. Sales proposal and contract approval.
10. Patient referral and care transition.

The examples illustrate the framework; they are not tests, proofs, required
implementations, or launch gates. The framework will include an SOP for
contributing additional examples.

### OQ-005 — What method maintains standards and SOPs?

**Decision:** Organizations create and maintain standards and SOPs through six
activities: Understand, Document, Validate, Approve, Use, and Improve.

See [ADR-006 — Standards maintenance method](../../decisions/0006-standards-maintenance-method.md).

### OQ-004 — What content must an SOP cover?

**Decision:** Every SOP must cover eight required content areas, but
organizations may use any layout that communicates them clearly. The standard
evaluates business meaning rather than template compliance.

See [ADR-005 — SOP content standard](../../decisions/0005-sop-content-standard.md).

### OQ-003 — What subjects must the framework standardize?

**Decision:** The framework standardizes six business concerns: Intent,
Responsibility, Work, Control, Assurance, and Learning.

See [ADR-004 — Six business concerns](../../decisions/0004-six-business-concerns.md).

### OQ-002 — Universal lifecycle or standards for existing lifecycles?

**Decision:** The framework defines minimum business standards that apply to
organizations' existing lifecycles and processes. It does not prescribe one
universal operating lifecycle.

See [ADR-003 — Standards for existing business lifecycles](../../decisions/0003-standards-for-existing-business-lifecycles.md).

### OQ-001 — What does AI-native change?

**Decision:** AI-native means the operating framework assumes people and AI may
both perform business work under the same standards and SOPs. It does not
require AI in every process or activity, and human accountability remains
explicit.

See [ADR-002 — AI-native business operations](../../decisions/0002-ai-native-business-operations.md).
