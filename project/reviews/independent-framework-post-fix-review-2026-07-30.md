## Application result

### Document choice

The relocation artifact should be a **time-bounded operating standard**, not an SOP. It governs one temporary program from authorization through transition and closure. Repeatable activities inside the program—inventory reconciliation, vendor handoff, daily coordination, change control, exception handling, and verification—receive a proportionate SOP-content check. That choice follows the explicit distinction between recurring SOPs and temporary standards, plans, playbooks, or procedures (`framework/operating-framework.md:200-215`).

The standard below is **illustrative and not domain-validated**. Every role, time window, gate, and operating assumption is fictional. It requires organizational safety, legal, records, privacy, workforce, facilities, contractual, and customer authorities to replace unresolved choices before use. The framework requires unresolved questions to have owners instead of being guessed (`framework/standards-maintenance-method.md:121-140`) and warns that documentation cannot prove a procedure lawful, safe, or professionally appropriate (`framework/sop-content-standard.md:330-335`).

# Illustrative Office Relocation Operating Standard

**Classification:** Time-bounded operating standard; not a recurring SOP<br>
**Status:** Illustrative draft; not approved; not domain-validated<br>
**Effective period:** Begins when the Move Sponsor approves the move charter; ends only when the closure gate is approved or an authorized extension is recorded<br>
**Illustrative time basis:** `T0` is the approved move window; planning begins at `T-8 weeks`; stabilization is targeted through `T+5 business days`; closure is targeted by `T+10 business days`

### Move mandate

Relocate one business office without preventable harm, unacceptable interruption of critical work, loss or uncontrolled exposure of records, loss of physical assets, unauthorized vendor activity, or missed customer commitments. Establish an accountable transition so the new location becomes the verified operating location and every residual issue has a human owner.

In scope: move authorization, people and safety planning, operational continuity, records custody, asset control, vendor coordination, building access, move execution, customer-commitment protection, stabilization, old-site release, and closeout.

Out of scope unless separately authorized: site selection, lease negotiation, major construction, workforce-policy changes, records destruction, new technology programs, and long-term facilities operation after transition. These may be dependencies but do not become silently authorized by this standard.

### Fictional operating assumptions

| Assumption | Boundary |
|---|---|
| One leased office with approximately forty workers moves within one jurisdiction. | Illustrative only; actual staffing, geography, and legal requirements remain unresolved. |
| A single move window is used, with controlled old-site access available until `T+5`. | The organization must approve the real dates and recovery window. |
| Critical customer work can use an approved remote or alternate-location fallback. | Actual continuity capability and acceptable interruption are unresolved. |
| No hazardous materials, regulated facility, or specialist collection is present. | Discovery of one triggers qualified review and re-planning. |
| Current policies for safety, privacy, records, access, procurement, contracting, and retention exist. | If any required policy is missing, conflicting, or inaccessible, the dependent work pauses; the standard supplies no substitute. |

### Command and authority

| Human role | Responsibility and bounded authority |
|---|---|
| Move Sponsor | Accountable owner for the relocation outcome; approves charter, scope, material change, go/no-go, accepted residual risk, and closure within delegated authority. |
| Move Director | Maintains the integrated plan and control register, coordinates workstreams, records decisions, and escalates missing authority. May stop affected work but may not waive another authority's requirement. |
| Safety Authority | Owns interpretation of applicable safety requirements, approves safety readiness, may stop unsafe work, and authorizes safety-related resumption within authority. |
| Continuity Lead | Defines critical operations, fallback arrangements, recovery priority, and service verification; owns continuity exceptions. |
| Records Custodian | Defines records classification, custody, retention, authorized transport, and disposition; no record is destroyed without controlling authority. |
| Asset Custodian | Owns inventory, tagging, chain of custody, reconciliation, discrepancy treatment, and final asset acceptance. |
| Privacy and Security Authority | Approves access, confidential-material handling, restricted areas, and incident treatment within assigned authority. |
| Vendor Coordinator | Confirms approved scope, work orders, prerequisites, contacts, access, handoffs, performance evidence, and deviations. Cannot change price, scope, contract, or risk acceptance without authority. |
| Old-Site and New-Site Leads | Control local readiness, access, departure, arrival, environmental checks, and site handoffs. |
| People Lead | Coordinates accessibility, worker instructions, emergency information, schedules, and approved employee support. Does not interpret requirements outside assigned competence. |
| Department Coordinators | Validate local people, records, assets, dependencies, customer obligations, packing readiness, and resumed operation. |
| Transition Verifier | Reviews gate evidence and records acceptance or discrepancy. Required independence and separation of duty must be set by organizational policy. |

One person may hold multiple roles only when risk and separation-of-duty requirements permit it. Delegations and alternates must be recorded; timing pressure never creates authority.

AI may compare approved inventories, summarize status, draft schedules or messages from approved content, flag missing evidence, and organize issue or decision records. Every AI output is labeled unverified until a named human reviews its sources and accepts it. AI may not interpret law or policy, decide safety or accessibility, approve access, destroy records, change customer commitments, accept contractual or financial deviations, authorize go/no-go, stop or resume work independently, or attest completion. It must not invent missing facts, policy, thresholds, authority, or evidence.

### Controlled records and resume point

The Move Director maintains a relocation control register as the authoritative record of program state. Each item records its owner, current verified state, next action, due point, dependency, decision or approval needed, exception status, and evidence reference. The register does not override the authoritative source for the underlying fact.

Authoritative sources include the approved move charter and budget; approved site and occupancy records; current worker and accessibility information; the critical-service and customer-commitment register; records policy and inventory; asset register; approved vendor agreements and work orders; building, safety, access, privacy, security, and continuity requirements; and signed gate, decision, change, exception, and closure records.

A chat, recollection, draft, AI summary, or vendor assertion may prompt investigation but is not authority. Missing, conflicting, stale, or inaccessible input is recorded as blocked, assigned to its source owner, and stops dependent decisions. Interrupted work resumes from the last verified register state—not from memory.

### Workstream rules

#### People and safety

- A qualified human authority identifies applicable occupational, building, fire, emergency, accessibility, lifting, traffic, and incident requirements.
- Participants receive approved duties, access boundaries, emergency instructions, reporting routes, and stop-work authority before their move activity.
- No person enters an unapproved area, performs specialist work outside competence, bypasses required protective measures, or retrieves property from an unsafe area.
- An injury, credible hazard, unsafe condition, occupancy failure, or unavailable emergency arrangement stops affected work and invokes the organization's approved response.

#### Operational continuity and customer commitments

- The Continuity Lead identifies critical work, minimum staffing, dependencies, approved fallback, restoration order, verification method, and accountable service owner.
- The customer-commitment register records each time-sensitive obligation, owner, due point, relocation impact, mitigation, approved message, and evidence of fulfillment.
- Customer communication is sent only by an authorized role using approved facts. No participant or AI creates a new promise, changes a deadline, or conceals a known service risk.
- If critical work cannot meet its approved continuity requirement, the affected move wave stops or reverts to the approved fallback while the accountable authority decides.

#### Records and information

- The Records Custodian classifies what moves, what remains, who may access it, the required custody method, and the authoritative destination.
- Containers or transfers carry an identifier, source, destination, custodian, contents classification, departure acceptance, arrival acceptance, and discrepancy state.
- Confidential records receive minimum-necessary access and approved physical or informational protection. Records destruction, retention changes, or unapproved copying are prohibited.
- A broken custody control, missing protected record, or uncontrolled exposure triggers containment, incident escalation, evidence preservation, and qualified recovery.

#### Physical assets and site readiness

- The Asset Custodian establishes the approved baseline, tag or identifier, condition, custodian, transport wave, destination, and acceptance result.
- Old-site release does not occur until assets, keys, credentials, leased items, and residual obligations are reconciled or accepted by proper authority.
- New-site acceptance covers safe access, utilities and environmental conditions, emergency arrangements, accessibility, secure areas, essential workspaces, receiving capacity, and remaining limitations.
- Missing or damaged items remain open; a signed carrier handoff proves transfer activity, not correct receipt.

#### Vendors and buildings

- One Vendor Coordinator is the official route for instructions and handoffs. Vendors act only within approved agreements, work orders, access, timing, and safety boundaries.
- Each vendor handoff carries current scope, location, schedule, prerequisites, contacts, acceptance criteria, protected-information limits, exception route, and evidence requirement.
- Scope, price, schedule, subcontractor, access, or method deviations enter change control before use. Urgency is not authorization.
- Vendor completion requires receiving-owner acceptance and discrepancy resolution or accountable residual ownership; attendance or invoice submission alone is insufficient.

### Coordination, decisions, and handoffs

From `T-10 business days` through operational acceptance, the Move Director holds an approved daily coordination review. Each workstream reports the current gate, last verified result, next action, dependency, decision needed, exception, and evidence reference. Material decisions record the question, options, affected commitments, consulted authorities, decision owner, decision, reason, effective point, and consequences.

Every handoff identifies the item or work package, sending owner, receiving owner, current verified state, criteria, evidence, open limitations, next action, and due point. The receiver accepts, rejects with reason, or escalates; silence is not acceptance.

A proposed change records its reason, affected people and commitments, safety and control impact, schedule and cost effect, dependencies, rollback or fallback, required authorities, decision, and communication. No coordinator can convert missing approval into an implied decision.

### Deviation, stop, containment, and recovery

Stop affected work when any of the following is true:

- immediate or credible safety risk exists;
- required occupancy, access, emergency, or specialist approval is absent;
- protected records or sensitive assets are missing, exposed, or outside custody;
- a vendor acts outside authorized scope or prerequisites;
- a critical continuity fallback is unavailable;
- a required gate approval or accountable owner is missing;
- customer commitments would be changed without authority;
- evidence appears false or cannot support the claimed state; or
- continuing may violate policy, law, contract, or professional direction.

On stop, protect people first; contain the affected area, material, access, or service; freeze dependent work; preserve the last verified state and evidence; notify the Move Director and owning authority; record impact and required decision; and use an approved fallback where one exists.

Recovery may retain the old site, use approved remote operation, hold protected items in controlled custody, restore the last approved scope, return a shipment when authorized, rebuild a manifest from retained evidence, reschedule a move wave, or use an approved alternate provider. These are illustrative options, not automatic authority. Resume only when the authority that owns the stop condition and the Move Director record that the cause is resolved or a bounded limitation is accepted, evidence is current, dependencies are rechecked, and affected participants are informed.

### Acceptance, evidence, and closeout

The relocation is complete only when:

- the new location is approved for safe and authorized use;
- critical operations and customer commitments are verified or have an explicitly accepted, bounded exception;
- records and physical assets reconcile to authoritative inventories or have contained discrepancies with accountable owners;
- required logical and physical access is correct;
- vendor deliverables and receiving handoffs are accepted;
- old-site release is authorized;
- incidents, exceptions, changes, and residual obligations are recorded;
- intended recipients can rely on the stated transition status; and
- the Move Sponsor approves closure after proportionate verification.

Evidence includes the charter, role and delegation record, source baselines, risk and issue register, integrated schedule, gate approvals, safety and accessibility reviews, continuity and service checks, customer-commitment status, approved communications, records and asset manifests, custody and receiving acknowledgments, vendor authorizations and acceptance, site approvals, incidents and recovery records, old-site release, closure decision, and lessons record.

Each control owner verifies the state within their authority. Department Coordinators verify local readiness. The Transition Verifier checks gate claims and discrepancies. The Move Sponsor accepts the integrated business outcome. Evidence retention location and period come from approved policy and law; this illustrative standard does not invent them.

### Keeping this standard current during the program

The Move Director maintains this version until closure. Review occurs at each gate and immediately after a safety incident, privacy or security event, missing record or asset, continuity failure, missed customer commitment, material vendor deviation, failed handoff, repeated exception, authority change, or evidence that the move plan no longer produces the intended outcome.

A material revision records its reason, evidence, affected roles and commitments, reviewers, approver, effective point, transition conditions, communication, and superseded guidance. The authorities responsible for affected requirements approve the revision. At closure, the Move Sponsor assigns any reusable recurring activity to an organizational owner for separate validation and approval as an SOP; temporary use does not silently create a recurring standard.

## Phase 3 — Contribution and appeal governance

For the simulated material clarification rejected as “already addressed”:

1. **Initiation:** The contributor uses the same designated contribution channel, labels the item as an appeal, and identifies the disputed contribution and decision (`CONTRIBUTING.md:393-407`). No current channel is named, so this is documented procedure rather than an executable external route today.
2. **Appeal contents:** Disputed contribution and decision; appeal ground; supporting evidence; requested resolution. The accepted ground specifically includes overlooked relevant evidence or an accepted framework requirement (`CONTRIBUTING.md:395-407`).
3. **Receipt and decision:** The framework maintainer records and acknowledges the appeal, preserves the original decision, and routes it to the founding steward, future governing body, or delegated appeal authority (`CONTRIBUTING.md:409-412`). A maintainer decision goes to the steward or future governing body. If the steward made the disputed decision and no broader body exists, the steward performs documented reconsideration with an uninvolved reviewer when practical and records that governance limitation (`GOVERNANCE.md:167-182`).
4. **Available dispositions:** Uphold, Revise, Reopen, or Defer (`CONTRIBUTING.md:414-420`).
5. **Preservation and communication:** The original decision remains; the appeal record identifies deciding authority, review scope, evidence considered, reason, date, and resulting action. The appeal and original remain visible together, and the contributor and affected maintainers receive the disposition (`CONTRIBUTING.md:409-425`; `GOVERNANCE.md:173-182`).
6. **Effect while pending:** The appeal does not authorize the proposed change and does not suspend safety, rights, privacy, or other containment (`CONTRIBUTING.md:421-425`).
7. **Current operational state:** External intake is **not operational**. The SOP names no destination and expressly says intake remains non-operational until the destination, acknowledging maintainer, receipt confirmation, and alternate route are published (`CONTRIBUTING.md:331-347`). Governance states the same pre-publication gate (`GOVERNANCE.md:162-165`), and the disposition records that no public channel is configured (`project/reviews/independent-framework-test-disposition-2026-07-30.md:53-58`).
8. **Before operational status can be claimed:** Publish the exact intake destination, responsible acknowledging maintainer, method for confirming the contribution was recorded, and alternate route; approve the required licensing and contribution terms before accepting external material for public distribution (`CONTRIBUTING.md:336-347,445-446`). No substitute intake channel is invented here.

## Direct answers

- **Could the framework be applied without an author briefing?** **Yes.** The core alone was sufficient to choose the document form and draft a complete operating standard.
- **Was the temporary-work document choice clear?** **Yes.** The current text directly distinguishes recurring SOPs from time-bounded forms (`framework/operating-framework.md:209-215`).
- **Could a domain-native structure preserve completeness?** **Yes.** The relocation standard and Example 04 both do so; the traceability tables make the coverage reviewable without dictating the operating layout.
- **Is the appeal procedure usable from the documentation alone?** **Not end to end today.** Its logic is complete, but external initiation cannot be performed from the repository alone until a contribution channel is named.
- **Does the repository honestly represent that public intake is not yet operational?** **Yes.** The controlling SOP, Governance, and disposition say so explicitly; the root status page could surface it more directly but does not claim that intake is operational.
- **Did the framework expose missing business decisions rather than encourage invention?** **Yes.** It requires assumptions and unresolved questions to remain visible and assigned, and it does not fill gaps with false authority (`framework/standards-maintenance-method.md:121-140`; `framework/sop-content-standard.md:256-268`).
- **Did it remain independent of technology?** **Yes.** Technology may support organizational procedures but does not define framework requirements (`framework/operating-framework.md:254-267`).
- **Single most important remaining improvement:** Reorganize one additional, substantively different example around its domain's natural work so the collection demonstrates structural freedom as a pattern rather than a lone exception.
