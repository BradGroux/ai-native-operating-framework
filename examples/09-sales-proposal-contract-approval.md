# Example 09: Sales Proposal and Contract Approval

## Example Record

**Provenance:** Generalized — synthesized from common business-to-business
proposal, pricing, contracting, and delivery-handoff patterns without
representing a specific company, customer, deal, product, or agreement.<br>
**Review status:** Illustrative; not domain-validated.<br>
**Draft contributor:** Framework drafting assistant, under Brad Groux's direction.<br>
**Responsible maintainer:** Framework maintainer.<br>
**Publication-safety note:** No real customer information, pricing, margin,
terms, negotiation, security response, contract, signature, or commercial
strategy is represented.<br>
**Review triggers:** Material framework change, sales, finance, delivery,
security, privacy, or legal review, a harmful ambiguity discovered in the
example, or change to the scenario assumptions.

## Scenario Overview

A business-to-business provider responds to customer opportunities that may
require a proposal, pricing, security or privacy statements, negotiated terms,
and a signed agreement before delivery begins.

The fictional provider maintains:

- an approved offering and claims catalog;
- a pricing and discount authority schedule;
- standard contract terms and a legal deviation guide;
- approved security, privacy, service, and compliance statements;
- delivery capacity and solution-approval practices; and
- a signature authority schedule.

The Account Executive owns the customer relationship but cannot independently
approve nonstandard price, legal terms, security commitments, delivery scope,
or signature. The Deal Owner coordinates one version of the proposed
commitment through review, negotiation, execution, and operational handoff.

# SOP: Develop, Approve, and Handoff a Customer Proposal and Contract

**Accountable owner:** Chief Commercial Officer<br>
**Process manager:** Commercial Operations Director<br>
**Approval status:** Approved for inclusion as illustrative; not approved for operational use<br>
**Review cycle:** Semiannually and upon a listed review trigger

## 1. Purpose, Scope, and Expected Outcome

This SOP ensures that customer proposals and contracts describe an understood
need, an achievable and supportable offering, authorized pricing and terms,
truthful organizational claims, and a commitment that delivery, finance, and
support can accept.

It covers:

- opportunity and customer-requirement intake;
- solution and delivery definition;
- pricing and commercial approval;
- proposal and response development;
- contract review and negotiation;
- final commitment and signature;
- handoff to delivery, billing, support, and obligation owners; and
- retained decision evidence.

It does not define lead generation, sales qualification strategy, product
roadmaps, legal doctrine, revenue accounting, tax treatment, detailed security
assessment, or delivery execution.

The expected outcome is either an authorized executed agreement with an
accepted operational handoff, or a documented decision not to proceed. Sending
a proposal or obtaining a signature alone is not completion.

## 2. Roles, Responsibilities, and Authority

| Role | Responsibility and authority |
|---|---|
| Chief Commercial Officer | Accountable for commercial governance, material exceptions, and this SOP. |
| Account Executive | Owns customer discovery, relationship, communication, opportunity facts, and coordination of customer decisions; cannot exceed delegated commitment authority. |
| Deal Owner | Maintains the authoritative deal record, integrated review, version control, approvals, dependencies, negotiation positions, and handoff. |
| Solution and Delivery Owner | Confirms scope, assumptions, dependencies, responsibilities, acceptance, capacity, feasibility, support, and delivery risk. |
| Product or Offering Owner | Confirms approved capabilities, roadmap statements, usage boundaries, and product commitments. |
| Pricing and Finance Authority | Approves price, discount, margin, payment, credit, currency, tax-routing, and financial exceptions within authority. |
| Legal Authority | Owns legal interpretation, contract deviations, negotiation positions, liability, rights, and approved legal language. |
| Security, Privacy, Compliance, or Risk Authority | Approves claims, questionnaires, obligations, controls, and exceptions within their domain. |
| Signature Authority | Executes the final agreement for the provider within delegated corporate authority. |
| Contract Administrator | Preserves the executed agreement, obligations, dates, notices, amendments, and authoritative version. |
| Delivery, Billing, Support, and Obligation Owners | Accept the final handoff and perform post-signature commitments. |

One person may hold several roles when authority and risk permit, but the
Account Executive does not self-approve their own out-of-policy price, term, or
capability promise.

AI may help summarize customer material, compare versions, draft from approved
content, identify missing approvals, or organize obligations. Responsible
owners validate its output. AI may not invent capability, fabricate evidence,
accept terms, make customer commitments, approve exceptions, or sign.

## 3. Trigger, Prerequisites, Inputs, and Authoritative Sources

This procedure begins when the provider decides to pursue a customer request
that may produce a proposal, quote, order, statement of work, contract,
amendment, renewal, or other binding commitment.

Prerequisites are:

- an identifiable customer and authorized customer contact;
- a named Account Executive and Deal Owner;
- a documented customer need and decision process;
- an initial offering, timing, value, and risk hypothesis;
- known proposal or negotiation deadlines;
- confidentiality and information-sharing status; and
- an authoritative deal record.

Authoritative sources are:

1. customer-issued requirements, questions, drafts, and clarifications;
2. approved offering, capability, claim, pricing, and policy records;
3. current delivery capacity, dependencies, estimates, and acceptance criteria
   from responsible owners;
4. approved legal forms and deviation positions;
5. approved security, privacy, compliance, accessibility, insurance, and
   service representations;
6. delegated pricing, risk, exception, and signature authority;
7. the negotiated final agreement; and
8. the deal record for current scope, version, decisions, approvals,
   commitments, and handoff state.

A prior proposal, marketing statement, informal message, or AI-generated answer
is not authority for a current customer commitment.

## 4. Procedure

### A. Register and Qualify the Commitment

The Account Executive and Deal Owner record:

- customer identity and decision participants;
- business need, desired outcome, and success basis;
- required scope, users, locations, data, integrations, service, support, and
  timing;
- budget, procurement, security, legal, and approval process;
- required response format and deadline;
- known competitors or alternatives only when lawfully known;
- confidentiality and information restrictions;
- material unknowns and assumptions; and
- the decision to pursue, defer, or decline.

The decision to pursue considers strategic fit, delivery feasibility,
commercial value, risk, and opportunity cost. Qualification does not authorize
a customer promise.

### B. Define the Proposed Solution and Delivery Boundary

The Solution and Delivery Owner works with Product or Offering Owners to define:

- included and excluded capabilities and services;
- customer and provider responsibilities;
- assumptions and dependencies;
- deliverables, milestones, acceptance, and change control;
- data, access, security, privacy, compliance, and support needs;
- staffing, capacity, third parties, locations, and timing;
- service levels and remedies only from approved positions;
- implementation and operational handoff; and
- known limitations and future items that are not committed.

The Account Executive tests the proposal against the customer's need. The Deal
Owner records unresolved gaps rather than converting them into implied scope.

Roadmap or future-capability statements require Product or Offering Owner
approval and must state whether they are a commitment, intention, dependency, or
excluded possibility.

### C. Build and Approve Pricing

Pricing and Finance Authority reviews:

- quantities, term, price basis, discounts, credits, and escalation;
- implementation, support, pass-through, travel, or usage charges;
- currency, payment timing, billing triggers, and credit;
- cost, capacity, margin, cash, tax-routing, and accounting considerations;
- dependencies between price and scope or customer responsibility; and
- exception authority.

Every departure from approved pricing identifies the reason, business value,
financial effect, approver, expiration, and condition. Participants may not
relabel or split consideration to avoid an approval threshold.

### D. Prepare the Proposal or Customer Response

The Deal Owner assembles content from approved owners. The response:

- answers the customer's stated need directly;
- distinguishes standard capability, configured scope, custom work,
  dependency, assumption, and exclusion;
- uses current approved organizational, product, security, privacy,
  compliance, accessibility, insurance, and customer-reference claims;
- aligns price, scope, timing, acceptance, service, and legal positions;
- identifies validity period and customer action;
- avoids unsupported superlatives, guarantees, certifications, or future
  promises; and
- carries an identifiable version.

Owners review only the sections within their competence, while the Deal Owner
checks the complete proposal for contradictions.

### E. Review and Approve Deviations

Before external release or agreement, the Deal Owner identifies deviations from
approved:

- offering and delivery;
- pricing and financial position;
- legal terms;
- security, privacy, data, compliance, accessibility, or insurance position;
- service level, warranty, indemnity, liability, remedy, or termination;
- customer reference, publicity, confidentiality, or intellectual property;
- staffing, timeline, acceptance, support, or dependency; and
- signature or entity authority.

Each deviation goes to its responsible authority with customer need, proposed
position, operational effect, alternatives, and recommendation. Approval states
the exact language or boundary, conditions, expiration, and linked concessions.

Silence, participation in a meeting, or approval of another section is not
approval of a deviation.

### F. Negotiate Under Controlled Authority

The Account Executive leads customer communication. Legal Authority controls
legal positions; Pricing and Finance controls financial positions; Solution,
Product, Security, Privacy, and other owners control their respective
commitments.

After each material exchange, the Deal Owner:

1. preserves the received and proposed versions;
2. identifies changed scope, price, terms, obligations, and dependencies;
3. routes new deviations;
4. confirms the approved negotiation position;
5. updates the decision and concession record; and
6. prevents an old or partially approved version from becoming the final
   agreement.

No one makes an off-record side promise. A material oral clarification is
incorporated into the controlled record before signature.

### G. Conduct Final Commitment Review and Signature

The Deal Owner assembles the final commitment package:

- final proposal, order, statement of work, agreement, and incorporated
  documents;
- customer and provider legal entities;
- scope, price, term, timing, acceptance, support, and responsibilities;
- all deviations and approvals;
- security, privacy, compliance, insurance, and data obligations;
- operational, billing, reporting, renewal, termination, and notice
  obligations;
- unresolved risks accepted by proper authority;
- delivery and capacity confirmation; and
- signature routing.

Legal Authority confirms legal form and version. Pricing and Finance confirms
economics. Solution and Delivery confirms deliverability. Specialist owners
confirm obligations. Signature Authority signs only the final approved version.

Any change after final review reopens affected approvals.

### H. Handoff the Executed Commitment

The Contract Administrator verifies signatures, dates, entities, incorporated
documents, and authoritative version, then records the agreement and key
obligations.

The Deal Owner leads a handoff to Delivery, Billing, Support, Customer Success,
and other obligation owners. The handoff includes:

- customer outcome and stakeholders;
- exact scope, exclusions, assumptions, and responsibilities;
- price, billing, acceptance, service, support, and milestone terms;
- security, privacy, data, compliance, insurance, reporting, and notice duties;
- commitments made during negotiation;
- risks, dependencies, change-control path, and escalation;
- source documents and approvals; and
- named owners and dates.

Each receiving owner accepts, rejects with reason, or escalates the handoff.
Sales activity is not complete until the operational commitment has an owner.

## 5. Policies, Controls, Approvals, and Risks

Controls include:

- one authoritative deal and version record;
- approved sources for capability and organizational claims;
- clear scope, assumptions, exclusions, dependencies, and customer duties;
- delegated pricing, legal, specialist, risk, and signature authority;
- traceable deviations and concessions;
- protected customer and provider confidential information;
- final cross-functional commitment review;
- no side promises;
- authoritative executed agreement; and
- obligation-level operational handoff.

Key risks include selling unavailable capability, hidden custom work, infeasible
timing, incorrect price or margin, unauthorized discount, contradictory
documents, false security or compliance claims, excessive legal exposure,
privacy or data obligations the provider cannot meet, wrong entity or signer,
side commitments, and delivery discovering material terms after signature.

## 6. Exceptions, Escalation, Recovery, and Stop Conditions

- **Customer requirement is unclear:** Ask for clarification or state a bounded
  assumption; do not silently decide the customer's need.
- **Deadline precedes required review:** Negotiate timing, narrow the response,
  or decline; urgency does not create authority.
- **Requested capability is unavailable:** Offer an approved alternative,
  explicitly exclude it, or seek Product and Delivery authority for a real
  commitment.
- **Pricing and scope no longer align:** Re-price or re-scope and reopen affected
  approvals.
- **Legal or specialist positions conflict:** Hold the affected commitment and
  escalate to the authorities responsible for risk and commercial decision.
- **Customer changes the final version:** Compare changes, stop signature, and
  repeat affected review.
- **Wrong version signed:** Do not begin unsupported performance; notify Legal
  and Contract Administration and execute an authorized correction, amendment,
  or decision.
- **Delivery rejects the handoff:** Keep the deal open, identify the
  unsupported commitment, and escalate to commercial and delivery authority.
- **Post-signature side promise appears:** Record it, avoid acting without
  authority, and route it through formal change or dispute handling.

Stop proposal release, negotiation, signature, or delivery handoff when a
material claim is unsupported, required approval is absent, the version is
uncertain, protected information is mishandled, the agreement is not
deliverable, or the signer lacks authority.

## 7. Completion, Verification, and Evidence

A successful deal process is complete when:

- the customer's need and final provider commitment are coherent;
- final scope, price, terms, entities, signatures, and incorporated documents
  are verified;
- all deviations and residual risks have proper approval;
- the authoritative executed version is retained;
- obligations and dates are identified;
- Delivery, Billing, Support, and other owners accept the handoff;
- customer communication reflects the executed commitment; and
- unresolved actions have accountable owners and deadlines.

A declined or lost opportunity is complete when the disposition, material
commitments, protected records, and follow-up obligations are closed
appropriately.

The Deal Owner verifies integrated completion. Contract Administration verifies
execution and retention. Each approving authority verifies its domain.
Receiving operational owners verify the handoff they accept.

Evidence includes customer requirements, approved solution, estimates, pricing,
financial analysis, proposal versions, claims sources, deviation and concession
records, domain approvals, negotiation history, final commitment package,
signatures, executed agreement, obligation record, and handoff acceptance.

## 8. Review, Approval, and Change History

The Chief Commercial Officer owns this SOP. Commercial Operations gathers
approval exceptions, cycle outcomes, delivery disputes, margin results,
customer feedback, obligation failures, and practitioner feedback.

Review occurs semiannually and sooner after:

- an unauthorized commitment, discount, term, claim, or signature;
- a material delivery, billing, support, privacy, security, legal, or customer
  dispute traced to sales or contracting;
- repeated late-stage approval or handoff failure;
- changed offerings, pricing, authority, law, or risk appetite;
- audit or control findings; or
- a relevant framework change.

Material revisions require Chief Commercial Officer approval and proportionate
review by Sales, Commercial Operations, Product, Delivery, Finance, Legal,
Security, Privacy, Contract Administration, and Support.

## Framework Annotation

| Concern | How the example expresses it |
|---|---|
| Intent | The outcome is an understood, deliverable, authorized commitment and accepted handoff—not a sent proposal or signature alone. |
| Responsibility | Relationship, deal coordination, solution, product, price, legal, specialist, signature, contract, and delivery authorities remain explicit. |
| Work | The procedure links customer need, solution, pricing, response, deviation review, negotiation, final approval, signature, obligation capture, and handoff. |
| Control | Approved claims, version control, deviation authority, concession history, signature authority, no-side-promise rule, and stop conditions bound commercial judgment. |
| Assurance | Cross-functional final review, executed-version verification, obligation records, and receiving-owner acceptance support the commitment. |
| Learning | Unauthorized commitments, disputes, handoff failures, outcomes, audits, and practitioner feedback trigger changes to the operating standard. |

The annotation explains the example; it does not add framework requirements.

## Domain-Specific Boundary

The sales roles, offering catalog, pricing schedule, legal playbook, deal record,
deviation review, negotiation method, signature routing, and delivery handoff
are choices for this fictional business-to-business scenario. The framework
does not prescribe sales stages, pricing models, contract language, revenue
practices, or these roles.

This example is not sales, pricing, revenue-recognition, tax, security, privacy,
contract, procurement, or legal advice. Organizations must use their own
offerings, authority, professional review, law, customer obligations, risk
appetite, and delivery model.
